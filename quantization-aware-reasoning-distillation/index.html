Quantization-aware reasoning distillation

Squeeze a reasoning model to low bit-widths and it does not simply get worse. It misjudges how long to think. This page is the working record of an independent paper I started in September 2026.

Size60.0 MB

Where the size goes

30M weights at FP16 is 60 MB. At Q4_K_M it is 16.9 MB, not 15 MB — the extra comes from the per-block scales every real format carries. The instrument uses published bits-per-weight, not the nominal bit count.

The question

A reasoning model decides, without being told, how many steps to spend on a problem. Quantization changes the weights that decision comes out of. My question is deliberately narrow: does low-bit quantization move chain-of-thought length away from the length a problem actually needs, and does it move in a consistent direction?

The failure has two sides. On hard problems the chain is cut short, and the model commits early to an answer it has not earned. On easy problems it runs long, spending steps on something it had already solved. The first costs accuracy. The second costs accuracy and money.

Both look the same from the outside — a number on a benchmark — which is part of why the effect is easy to miss.

In one line

Does low-bit quantization miscalibrate how long a model reasons, and can distillation put the calibration back?

Status

Drafting. Introduction first. No results to report yet.

Why it is worth asking

Low-bit inference is how a model reaches hardware that is not a datacentre: a phone, a single-board computer, a microcontroller. I came to the question from the other direction, while trying to get a 30-million-parameter model I wrote from scratch onto hardware that fits in a pocket. Quantization is the step that makes that possible, and it is the step where the model's behaviour started to shift in ways the accuracy number did not explain.

Most quantization work measures the quality of the final answer. Much less of it measures the shape of the reasoning that produced the answer. If the reasoning budget is miscalibrated, a benchmark score can hold steady while what the model is doing underneath changes a great deal — and the gap shows up later, on the problems nobody tested.

What I am measuring

The models, the bit-widths, how the length a problem needs is defined, the metric and the distillation setup are still being written. There are no results on this page because there are none yet. When there are, they go here with the method that produced them, including the runs that did not work.
