ATLAS collision predictor

A 3D viewer for proton–proton collisions in the style of CERN's ATLAS detector, with a C++ engine that scores each event as signal or background. Trained on real ATLAS Open Data.

The write-up

Why

Most collisions at the LHC are boring. Higgs production is roughly one event in ten billion, so the real problem is a needle in a haystack: does this pile of tracks and energy deposits look like the signal, or like the background?

What I built

The whole pipeline. Real ATLAS events flattened to one row each, a small network trained from scratch in NumPy with hand-written backprop, and its weights exported to a header-only C++17 engine with no machine-learning library of any kind. Tests prove both sides agree bit for bit, and the viewer re-runs the model every frame while you fly around the detector.

How it did

ROC AUC 0.808 on 83,868 held-out real events, against a 0.5 baseline, at 262 nanoseconds per event. The number is held down on purpose: the schema has no slot for the hadronic tau, the best feature in the dataset, and the write-up says so rather than hiding it.
